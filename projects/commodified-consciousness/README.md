
# Commodified Consciousness

### An Interdisciplinary Investigation of Consciousness, Value, and Subjectivity

## Thesis 
The object cannot be understood exclusively through its material properties or economic function.
Its significance emerges through the relations it enters into with subjects, other objects, and the symbolic structures of its social environment.
Consequently, the consumption of objects is also implicated in the constitution of subjectivity.

## Overview

An ongoing research project examining the relationship between consciousness, commodity relations, symbolic value, and the formation of subjectivity.

The project attempts to bring together political economy, continental philosophy, and psychoanalysis to investigate how objects, social relations, and systems of exchange participate in the construction of human experience.

## Central Questions

- How do objects acquire meaning beyond their material properties?
- How does symbolic and sign-value influence the perception of objects?
- How are subjectivity and identity constituted through social relations?
- What can psychoanalysis contribute to an understanding of commodity culture?
- How might contemporary consumer society require a reconsideration of traditional political economy? (Heavy emphasis on Baudrillard's Consumerism)
